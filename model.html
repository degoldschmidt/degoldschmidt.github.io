Context-dependent retrieval, as a playable model

The same smell can mean danger or nothing at all, depending on where the fly thinks it is. This is a cartoon of how a mushroom body could do that — running live in your browser.
{% include mbsim.html variant="full" %}

What you are looking at

The fly walks an endless grid of odour patches. Two things vary: the visuo-spatial context — the panorama along the top, green or blue — and the odour in the patch underfoot. Odour identity runs anti-diagonally across the grid, so it is decorrelated from position: “odor 1” is not simply “the left column”.

During training, odour 1 is punished, and only in the danger context. Nothing is punished in the safe context. That asymmetry is the whole experiment: it makes context an occasion setter. Context does not reverse what the odour means — it gates whether the memory is expressed at all.

Press danger or safe to override what the fly sees while leaving the world unchanged. The shock switches off automatically, because an override is a test trial. Watch the path: in the danger context the fly reverses at the border of every odour-1 patch; in the safe context it walks through the same patches without turning.

How it works

Odour and context converge on Kenyon cells. Each combination activates three pools: cells that code the odour regardless of context, cells that code the context regardless of odour, and conjunctive cells that fire only for that specific pairing. Conjunctive coding is the part that makes retrieval context-dependent, and it is biologically available — γd and ventral-accessory-calyx Kenyon cells carry visual input.

Learning is dopamine-gated depression of Kenyon-cell → output-neuron synapses, restricted to cells that were active when the shock arrived. Punishment depresses the approach-driving output neuron rather than writing in avoidance, so a naive fly reads out exactly zero — indifferent — and avoidance appears as that balance tips negative.

Nothing in the locomotion is goal-directed. The fly runs roughly straight and turns in discrete saccades; a negative memory just makes it turn more, and more sharply, at a patch border. Avoidance is emergent, not programmed.

Re-entering a punished patch without the shock relaxes the weights back toward baseline — extinction, or memory re-evaluation. Switch the shock off in the controls above and watch the matrix climb back toward zero.

What this is not

It is not a result. There are no fitted parameters and no data anywhere in it; every number on this page is produced by the equations, not measured. It is a way to make an idea manipulable, and to be explicit about what the idea actually predicts.

The plasticity rule follows the published mushroom-body literature — Aso et al. 2014, Hige et al. 2015, Cohn et al. 2015, Owald et al. 2015. The paradigm it simulates is shown in the figure on the projects page. Roughly 340 lines of vanilla JavaScript, no libraries: read the source.
