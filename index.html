Dennis Goldschmidt

I am a postdoctoral fellow in the Felsenberg Lab at the Friedrich Miescher Institute for Biomedical Research in Basel, Switzerland. I study how a fly’s memories are re-evaluated — and how what it sees decides which memories get used at all.

A smell that meant danger yesterday may mean nothing today, and a smell that means danger in one place may be safe in another. My work asks how the brain holds both of those facts at once: the neuronal circuit mechanisms of memory re-evaluation, and specifically context-dependent memory retrieval.

How I work on it

  • I build experimental systems for real-time closed-loop two-photon imaging, sensory stimulation, optogenetics and video tracking.
  • I measure and analyse fly brain activity during ongoing behaviour and learning.
  • I genetically manipulate activity in the neural circuitry of fly brains.
  • And I develop computing pipelines for automated data analysis and visualisation.
